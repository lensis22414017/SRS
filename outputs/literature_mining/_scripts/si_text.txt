S1.1 Chemical analysis
S1.1.1 Details on methods

All the soil samples (after drying and homogenization) were spiked with 20ng of 2,4,5,6-tetrachloro-m-xylene (TCmX), PCB 198 and PCB 209 as surrogate recovery standards and Soxhlet extracted with dichloromethane (DCM) for 36 h. Activated copper granules were added into the collection flask to eliminate elemental sulphur. Each extract was concentrated by a rotary evaporator and solvent-exchanged into hexane. Extracts were cleaned-up through a multilayer silica gel alumina column packed as follows: 1cm anhydrous sodium sulphate, 3cm 50% sulfuric acid silica, 3cm neutral silica gel (3% deactivated) and 1cm neutral alumina (3% deactivated) from top to bottom. OCPs were eluted with 20mL of a mixture of DCM/hexane (1:1, V/V), and then concentrated to 0.5 mL by using a gentle stream of nitrogen. A GPC column was applied to the final clean-up step. Bio-Beads S-X3 (6.5g) were used in a 15cm glass column (id=2 cm). Each concentrated sample was loaded and eluted with 55mL of a mixture DCM/hexane (1:1, V/V). The first 15mL fraction was discarded and the following 40mL fraction containing the OCPs and the recovery standards was collected. The elution was concentrated to 30μL and exchanged into dodecane by using a gentle stream of nitrogen again. 10 ng 13C-PCB 141 as internal standard was added into each samples before instrumental analysis. 
Total Organic Carbon (TOC) in soil samples were determined by an elemental analyzer (CHNS Vario Ei III, Elementar) after removal of carbonates with HCl.
The OCP standards and surrogates were purchased from AccuStandard Inc. (New Haven, USA). 13C-PCB 141 was purchased from Cambridge Isotope Laboratories (Andover, USA). Chromatographic purity hexane and dichloromethane (DCM) were purchased from Honeywell B&J (Morristown, USA). Anhydrous sodium sulfate (analytical grade, Guangzhou chemistry reagent factory, China) was pretreated in a muffle furnace at 450 °C for 12 h. Silica gel (100–200 mesh) and Neutral alumina (analytical grade) were purchased from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Before using, the neutral silica gel and neutral alumina were each Soxhlet-extracted with DCM for 48 h, and then baked for 12 h in 180 °C and 250 °C, respectively. Sulfuric acid silica gel (50% by mass concentrated sulfuric acid) was prepared by drop wise addition of concentrated H2SO4 (analytical grade, Beijing chemistry reagent factory, China) to dry silica gel (100–200 mesh). Bio-Beads S-X3 was purchased from Bio-Rad Laboratories Inc. (Hercules, USA). Before using, Bio-Beads S-X3 was immersed in DCM/hexane (1:1, V/V) for 24 h.
S1.1.2 Instrumental analysis
Analysis of OCPs
Thirteen compounds (a-endosulfan, b-endosulfan, HCB, Mirex, o,p’-DDT, pp’-DDT, o,p’-DDD, pp’-DDD, o,p’-DDE, pp’-DDE, CC, TC and Heptachlor) were detected in this study, they were measured by using an Agilent 7890/7000 GC-MS/MS, the mass spectrometer was an EI source (-70 eV) in the multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) mode. A HP-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm i.d. × 0.25 µm film thickness, Agilent, CA, USA) was installed to separate compounds. 1 µL of sample was injected in splitless mode. Helium was used as carrier gas at the flow rate of 1 mL/min. Collision induced dissociation (CID) gas and quench gas in collision cell were helium and nitrogen at the flow rate of 2.25 mL/min and 1.5 mL/min, respectively. The temperatures of transfer line, injector interface and ion source were set at 280 °C, 250 °C and 230 °C, respectively. The initial oven temperature was set at 80 °C for 0.5 min, 20 °C min-1 to 160 °C, then 4 °C/min to 240 °C, and finally 10 °C/min to 295 °C (held for 10 min). The scan time of all the segments ranged between 0.234 s and 0.255 s. In this study, the thermal degradation of DDT was checked by the identification and quantification of DDT monomeric standard. No other compounds were detected in our instrumental quantitative analysis, indicating DDT did not occur thermal degradation. The OCPs standard was injected once every ten samples to determine reproducibility of OCP analysis. If the measured DDT concentration is lower than 95% of the actual content of DDT standard, we clean the injector to avoid the inlet thermal degradation of DDT that are higher than 5%.
Chiral analysis
Determination of enantiomeric compositions of o,p'-DDT, trans-chlordane (TC), and cis-chlordane (CC) were performed on the Agilent GC-MS/MS with a BGB-172 column (15 m × 0.25 cm i.d. × 0.25 mm film thickness, BGB Analytik AG, Switzerland). The initial oven temperature was set at 90 °C for 1 min, 20 °C/min to 160 °C, then 2 °C/min to 210 °C (held for 5 min), and finally 20 °C/min to 230 °C (held for 10 min). The other GC and MS parameters were consistent with the analysis of achiral OCPs above.
The enantiomeric elution order of enantiomers for TC, CC and o,p'-DDT on the BGB column was (+)TC, (-)TC, (+)CC, (-)CC, (-)o,p’-DDT, (+)o,p’-DDT. Results were expressed as enantiomer fraction (EF), EF = peak areas of the (+)/ [(+) + (-)] enantiomers. To ensure peak purity, ion ratios for each compound were compared to the racemic standard (Ehrenstorfer Laboratories, Augsburg, Germany) which was run repeatedly during chiral analysis, and were always within 5%.
S1.2 Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC)
The stability of GC-MS/MS was checked daily by using OCPs standards. The Instrument Detection Limit (IDL) ranged from 0.0310-3 to 0.1910-3 ng, and the Method Detection Limit (MDL) was calculated by 3.36SDblank + averageblank, ranging from 0.1510-3 to 9.5710-3 ng/g, depending on congeners (Table S2). The surrogates described above were analyzed to determine procedural recoveries of OCPs. The relative standard deviation of the measurements of 20-pair repeated samples ranged from 5.91% to 10.1%.

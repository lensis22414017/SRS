Abstract
Cultivation-independent molecular approaches were used to investigate the phylogenetic composition of Archaea and the relative
abundance of phylogenetically defined groups of methanogens in the leachate of a closed municipal solid waste landfill. Cloning and
phylogenetic analysis of archaeal 16S rRNA gene sequences (16S rDNA) revealed that the landfill leachate harbored a diverse Archaea
community, with sequence types distributed within the two archaeal kingdoms of the Euryarchaeota and the Crenarchaeota. Of the
80 clones examined, 51 were phylogenetically associated with well-defined methanogen lineages covering two major methanogenic
phenotypes; 20 were related to Thermoplasma and were grouped with some novel archaeal rRNA gene sequences recently recovered from
various anaerobic habitats; finally, five belonged to Crenarchaeota and were not closely related to any hitherto cultivated species. Most of
the methanogen-like clones were affiliated with the hydrogenotrophic Methanomicrobiales and the methylotrophic and acetoclastic
Methanosarcinales. Quantitative oligonucleotide hybridization experiments showed that methanogens in the leachate accounted for only a
very small fraction of the total community (approximately 2%) and that Methanomicrobiales and Methanosarcinales constituted the
majority of the total methanogenic population.

1. Introduction
Being a simple and e¡ective biotechnological process,
anaerobic digestion has been exploited on a large scale
to reduce the pollution caused by organic wastes [1]. An-
aerobic processes involve the sequential breakdown of
complex organic compounds by several e¡ectively interact-
ing metabolic groups of microorganisms [2,3]. However,
the limitations of the traditional cultivation-based tech-
niques have restricted our knowledge of the microbiology
in anaerobic treatment systems. Much needs to be done to
identify and quantify the important microorganisms par-
ticipating in the terminal steps (e.g. methanogenesis) of
organic waste degradation. Molecular techniques have
proven to be e¡ective for studying prokaryotes in nature
without cultivation. Recently, several studies using 16S
rRNA gene (16S rDNA) sequence analysis on microbial
communities in anaerobic treatment systems have shown
that many of the species in these habitats remain to be
identi¢ed [4^7]. By the application of 16S rRNA-targeted
oligonucleotide probes, other investigations determined
the relative abundance of phylogenetically de¢ned groups
of key microorganisms (e.g. methanogens and sulfate-re-
ducing bacteria) in anaerobic digesters and related the
presence and activity of these microbial populations to
the system performance [8,9]. These studies have unprece-
dentedly provided detailed insights into the microbial di-
versity and activity of these poorly characterized ecosys-
tems.
In most countries, sanitary land¢lling remains an inte-
gral part of both existing and new strategies for municipal
solid waste (MSW) disposal. However, much of what is
known or assumed concerning anaerobic processes in
land¢lls has primarily come from investigations of anaer-
obic digesters. Although the microbial populations in

2. Materials and methods
2.1. MSW land¢ll, sample collection and chemical analysis
Gouzikeng Land¢ll is located in a suburb approximately
8 km east of Zhongshan City, Guangdong Province, P.R.
China and has a capacity of about 1 200 000 m3. Its oper-
ations started in 1982 and were ¢nished in 1996. A poor
topsoil cover was applied after its closure.
Samples of leachate oozing from the bottom of the
land¢ll were collected in 1-l screw-cap bottles and placed
immediately on ice for transport to the laboratory. All
treatments were in triplicate. Chemical analyses of the
leachate samples were performed as previously described
[10], following procedures described in [12]. The results of
these analyses are given in Table 1.

2.4. Quantitative oligonucleotide hybridization experiments
A set of oligonucleotide probes (S-F-Mbac-0310-a-A-22,

cies
in

original

[27,28].
Alternatively,
oligonucleotide probes can be used in dot-blot hybridiza-
tions to quantify the abundance of a certain rRNA in the
nucleic acids extracted directly from environmental sam-
ples [27]. Although data of relative rRNA abundance can-
not be directly translated into cell numbers, they represent
a reasonable measurement of the relative physiological

consideration in ecological studies. Therefore, we esti-

Given that full-scale land¢ll ecosystems are huge and
complex and that they are highly heterogeneous with re-
spect to waste composition, in¢ltration of water and the
presence of microorganisms, it is very di⁄cult to obtain
comprehensive and detailed information on the ecology of
the microbial populations and communities in situ. In or-
der to get a quick assessment of the methanogenic Ar-
chaea contributing to the waste stabilization process in
the land¢ll system, we started with a molecular survey of
the microbial assemblages in its e¥uent leachate. Leachate

complex mixture of organic, inorganic and bacteriological

degradation. Chemical parameters of land¢ll leachate

development of the mineralization process, and in combi-
nation with biogas analysis, they provide useful informa-
tion on the overall community activity and system perfor-
mance. Therefore, we believe that our present work,
within the limitations of the molecular technique involved,
has given us a useful insight into the methanogenic pop-
ulations within the full-scale land¢ll system and can be
used as a starting point for more detailed studies.

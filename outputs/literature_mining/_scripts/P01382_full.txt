Nitrogen input leads to the differential accumulation of polycyclic aromatic

A B S T R A C T   
Because of a shortage of water resources, sewage irrigation has become a popular management tool for farmland 
soil in arid areas of China; however, this has led to the accumulation of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) 
in soil. Soil is an important component of ecosystems, and nitrogen is an important nutrient required for plant 
growth. Nitrogen input can alter the physical, chemical, and biological processes in soil and thus lead to changes 
in soil organic matter and organic pollutants. However, whether these changes affect the accumulation of PAHs 
and whether such accumulation differs in the low-density fraction (LF) and high-density fraction (HF) of soil 
remains unclear. In this study, the response of PAHs in soil to nitrogen input (0, 100, 200, and 300 kg N ha−1 
yr−1, respectively), including differences in LF and HF, were investigated through field experiments in a typical 
sewage-irrigated area. The results showed that nitrogen input could increase the concentrations of PAHs in soil 
from (7.6 ± 1.1) × 103 to (10.4 ± 0.6) × 103 μg kg−1 and lead to striking differences between the LF ((5.06 ±
0.75) × 103 to (1.89 ± 0.18) × 103 μg kg−1) and HF ((2.54 ± 0.36) × 103 to (8.54 ± 0.44) × 103 μg kg−1). Given 
the significant increase in global nitrogen input, our findings have implications for the optimization and man­
agement of agricultural activities in sewage irrigation areas, such as soil investigation before fertilization, the use 
of soil improvers, and the improvement of soil planting measures.   
1. Introduction 
Farmland soil is critically important for the physical, chemical, and 
biological processes in terrestrial ecosystems (Wilson et al., 2019). Grain 
crops such as wheat and maize are critically dependent on farmland soil, 
and water and nitrogen are essential for ensuring their survival and 
growth (Tan et al., 2016; Pan et al., 2017). However, the growing global 
population, the increase in farmland area, and the increasing demand 
for irrigation water place a continuous strain on freshwater resources. 
To conserve scarce water resources, many countries have begun using 
industrial and domestic sewage to irrigate farmland (Agnellos Barbosa 
et al., 2017; Wu et al., 2017). In China, especially in arid areas in the 
north, water resources are scarce. Consequently, sewage has been used 
for irrigation in the farmland in the North China Plain for nearly 40 years 
(Meng et al., 2016; Yan et al., 2020a). Most studies have suggested that 
direct irrigation with sewage is one of the main sources of organic pol­
lutants in farmland soil. After years of accumulation, many refractory 
and persistent organic pollutants now pose a great threat to the soil 
ecological environment and human health (Wang et al., 2002; Khan 
et al., 2008). 
Agricultural soil also requires fertilization (Pan et al., 2017). Previ­
ous studies have shown that nitrogen input, especially the addition of 
nitrogen fertilizers, plays a crucial role in soil ecosystems, including 
maintaining soil nutrients levels and ensuring plant growth (Zhou et al., 
2018; Lu et al., 2020). However, some studies have suggested that ni­
trogen addition can even have negative effects, such as causing soil 
acidification and decreasing the stability of the soil biological commu­
nity (Janssens et al., 2010; Zhang et al., 2018). In addition, nitrogen 
input could affect the composition and activity of soil microbial com­
munity (Dai et al., 2018), and thus changes the bioavailability and 
degradation efficiency of organic pollutants (such as PAHs) in soil
